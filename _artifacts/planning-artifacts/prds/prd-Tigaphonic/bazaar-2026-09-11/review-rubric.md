# PRD Quality Review — Bazaar

## Overall verdict
This is a well-run PRD: the Vision (§1) states a sharp, non-generic thesis (recurring secondhand/serialized-inventory pain no generic package — including Lunar PHP by name — covers), and nearly every FR carries genuinely testable consequences rather than adjectives, with honest trade-off documentation (the partial-refund reversal in §4.3 FR-17 Notes, the deliberately-deferred anti-counterfeiting module in §6.2). The core inventory/order/return model is unusually well specified for an internal-first PRD. The two real risks are structural rather than strategic: the Reporting domain (FR-25) is declared in MVP scope (§6.1) yet has no testable specification at all, and the UJ↔FR "Realizes" cross-references around Return/Cancellation are scrambled (UJ-4, which narrates FR-13 almost verbatim, cites FR-15 instead; UJ-3 cites FR-13 and FR-14, neither of which its narrative touches) — a traceability defect that matters because this PRD explicitly feeds UX/architecture/story generation (§0).

## Decision-readiness — strong
Trade-offs are named with what was given up, not smoothed over. §4.3 FR-17 Notes states plainly: "Ini mencabut keputusan 'no partial refund' yang sebelumnya dikunci di sesi ini (mengikuti brief asli) — direvisi atas permintaan eksplisit user, dengan guardrail rentang Minimum/Maximum % sebagai penggantinya" — a documented reversal, not a silently updated decision. The one `[NOTE FOR PM]` callout (§6.2, anti-counterfeiting/verification module) sits at a real tension — "trade-off biaya vs risiko trust yang disadari, bukan kealpaan" — not a safe checkpoint. The counter-metric SM-C1 explicitly names the risk that chasing SM-1 could cause over-engineering that slows client integration, which is the opposite of a "balances everything" hedge. §9's Assumptions Index goes a step further than required, flagging one un-confirmed logical inference (Refund applying to Cancelled, not just Returned, orders) even though it isn't a tagged `[ASSUMPTION]` — genuine transparency about an unresolved edge.

No findings — this dimension does not need fixing.

## Substance over theater — strong
The Vision is not swappable into another PRD: it names a specific competitor gap ("Lunar PHP paling dekat, namun klaim headless-nya pincang karena tidak memiliki API resmi") and a specific mechanical challenge (1 unit = 1 listing, manual condition grading, atomic per-unit locking) rather than generic category language. NFRs are specific and backed: FR-5's "Reservasi Stock harus atomic di bawah concurrent load" is not boilerplate — it is unpacked into a concrete conditional-update mechanism in addendum §C. Five named individuals appear across the UJs (Andi, Sari, Rara, Budi, Dewi) — above the rubric's four-persona theater threshold on a raw count — but each is tied to specific FRs realized (install flow, maker-checker publish, order lifecycle, returns) rather than existing to look thorough; this reads as journey scaffolding for a genuinely multi-role internal tool, not persona theater.

No findings — the count is worth noting but does not indicate theater given what each persona does in the document.

## Strategic coherence — strong
The thesis in §1 (repeated from-scratch backend builds, with secondhand inventory as the specific uncovered case) is directly reflected in MVP scope: §6.1 headlines "Model inventory terpadu: Condition (New/Second) × Inventory Strategy (Pooled/Serialized)" as the centerpiece, not a buried feature. SM-1 ("Non-recurring defects") measures whether the exact pattern named in the Vision — "race condition stok, inkonsistensi status Order, celah reservasi" — stops recurring across client projects; this validates the thesis directly rather than measuring activity (no DAU/MAU-style proxy metric here). A counter-metric is named (SM-C1). The eight-domain MVP breadth is large for what's framed as an internal/dogfooding-first tool, but it matches the thesis: Tigaphonic already builds all eight domains per client today, so parity-with-current-practice is the actual bar, not gold-plating.

No findings.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
Most FRs are a model of this dimension — bounds instead of adjectives, with concrete numeric examples (FR-17's Refund range worked example: "total Rp100.000, Minimum 20% = Rp20.000, Maximum 80% = Rp80.000"). But one in-scope MVP domain has no testable content at all, and two other FRs are partially unspecified.

### Findings
- **high** Reporting (FR-25) has zero testable consequences despite being declared in-scope for MVP (§6.1) — Consequences read only "Reporting adalah domain read-only dengan dependency 1 arah" and "Daftar laporan spesifik & definisi metrik detail berada di luar cakupan PRD ini." §6.1 explicitly lists Reporting among "8 domain module dengan Filament admin UI penuh (FR-1–FR-29)." An engineer cannot build a single report from this FR. *Fix:* either drop Reporting from the in-scope domain list until at least one report is specified, or add a minimal testable slice (e.g., name the first report and its required fields) before treating it as MVP-committed.
- **medium** SEO metadata (FR-24, FR-29) and Reporting's field list are all deferred to the same two Open Questions (§8) — FR-24's only consequence beyond "attaches as a field" is "Field spesifik... belum ditentukan → lihat §8"; FR-29 ("Global SEO Defaults") has no consequence beyond the same pointer. This is honestly flagged (good scope honesty) but leaves two FRs and a sub-feature without a buildable definition. *Fix:* no urgent fix needed if these truly wait for UX/architecture per §8, but flag them as blocked-until-resolved in sprint planning rather than counted as done-ready FRs.

## Scope honesty — strong
§5 Non-Goals is substantive, not generic — each item carries a reason (e.g., "Algoritma alokasi otomatis split-shipment... tidak relevan di v1 (Item Pooled dibatasi 1 Warehouse per Item)"). §9's Assumptions Index confirms zero outstanding inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags, consistent with a spot-check of the body (none found), and additionally surfaces one non-tagged inference for transparency. Open-items density is appropriately low for a PRD heading toward build (2 Open Questions, 1 `[NOTE FOR PM]`, 0 outstanding assumptions) — this reads as a well-resolved coaching session rather than corner-cutting, since the low count sits alongside unusually dense per-FR testable detail.

### Findings
- **low** §5 (Non-Goals) and §6.2 (Out of Scope for MVP) overlap substantially with inconsistent permanence framing — e.g., "Multi-currency / pembayaran & pengiriman cross-border" appears in §5 (framed as a fixed Non-Goal) and again in §6.2 as "Multi-currency / cross-border — dideferred, belum ada kebutuhan klien saat ini" (framed as revisitable). A reader can't tell from §5 alone which of its items are permanent versus provisionally deferred. *Fix:* either merge the two sections or add an explicit rule distinguishing "permanent Non-Goal" from "MVP-deferred, revisit-eligible" items.
- **low** The title itself is an open item not tracked in §8 — line 9's "*Working title — confirm.*" is a live unresolved decision but doesn't appear in the Open Questions list. *Fix:* add it to §8 or resolve it.

## Downstream usability — adequate
The Glossary (§3) is rich (20 terms) and section 0 explicitly anchors downstream extraction to it. FR/UJ numbering is contiguous overall — all of FR-1 through FR-35 are used exactly once despite FR-30–35 being appended out of sequence into their proper domain sections (evidence of decisions added later in the Journey-led sync without renumbering damage). All four UJs have named protagonists; none float. The one real defect is in the UJ→FR traceability itself.

### Findings
- **high** UJ-3/UJ-4 "Realizes" cross-references are mismatched around Return/Cancellation — UJ-4 ("Dewi menangani pembatalan & retur") narrates the exact three-branch structure (A: no AWB yet / B: AWB registered, not shipped / C: physically shipped) that is FR-13 "Return & Cancellation," almost clause-for-clause, yet its Realizes line says "Realizes: FR-15" (CS Interaction Log) — FR-13 is not listed anywhere in UJ-4. Meanwhile UJ-3 ("Order dari checkout sampai selesai... jalur normal") lists "Realizes: FR-10, FR-11, FR-13, FR-14, FR-28, FR-30, FR-31, FR-32" — but UJ-3's narrative never touches Return/Cancellation (FR-13) or Promo (FR-14) at all. A downstream story-generation pass tracing UJ-4 to its FR would land on the wrong FR, and FR-13 — one of the more complex FRs in the document — ends up with no UJ correctly citing it. *Fix:* change UJ-4's Realizes line to FR-13 (optionally plus FR-15 if CS logging is meant to be implied), and remove FR-13/FR-14 from UJ-3's Realizes line.

## Shape fit — strong
This is not a single-operator internal tool — it has a developer-installer persona plus multiple distinct staff roles with maker-checker gates, so UJs are load-bearing rather than overhead, and the PRD invests in them exactly where the workflows are genuinely branchy (install: UJ-1; publish maker-checker: UJ-2; full order lifecycle: UJ-3; returns/cancellation: UJ-4). It correctly skips UJs for domains that are simple capability specs (Content, Reporting, Notification, Global Settings) rather than forcing journey narrative where none is warranted. The PRD also tracks its lineage against the source brief accurately (addendum §A–I cite "source: brief §X" per principle and flag what changed this session, e.g. "Refurbished, dicabut oleh keputusan sesi ini"), which matters given this is built atop an existing draft brief.

### Findings
- **low** Refund (FR-17) is a maker-checker flow of comparable complexity to Item-publish (UJ-2) and Return/Cancellation (UJ-4) — including a session-level policy reversal (partial refund) — but has no dedicated UJ; it's only referenced in passing from FR-13's prose and the Assumptions Index. *Fix:* not necessarily required, but consider a short UJ for Staff Finance submitting/an Approval Role approving a Refund Request, since its guardrail logic (Min/Max % range) is exactly the kind of branchy, easy-to-get-wrong flow UJs elsewhere in this PRD were used to pin down.
